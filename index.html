What the patient and prescriber run into

Where the leverage sits

  • From the essay

    A blank field reads as a failed criterion

    When a prescriber writes in the margin instead of ticking a box, extraction returns nothing and the rules engine treats that absence as failure. Annotating each question together with its answer fixed more than any prompt change did.

  • From the essay

    Every denial goes to a person

    Approval letters could be drafted for a reviewer to check and send. Denials were always reviewed in full, because the likeliest cause of a denial was a document that failed extraction.

  • From the memo

    One supplier means no easy switch

    When a single-supplier drug goes short, there is no interchangeable product to dispense. The patient needs a different drug, strength or form and a new prescription, and a restricted alternative needs its own authorization.

  • From the essay

    Faster review, same override rate

    Review time fell while override rates held flat. Complex cases were re-reviewed blind by a second pharmacist, and a growing golden set was rerun monthly to catch drift.

  • From the essay

    Ingestion sets the ceiling

    About 95% of requests arrived by fax or email, and roughly 30% of those extracted cleanly enough to automate. The model was the easy part.

  • From the essay

    Sequence on form structure

    We rolled out by clinical complexity. The better order starts with the drug whose form has the most checkboxes, the most stable criteria and the least free text.

  • From the essay

    Price the retrain

    Every P&T decision or guideline revision changes a form, and extraction has to relearn it from live submissions. One guideline change took about two quarters to recover from.

  • From Access Fragility

    Find the buyer before the build

    Two partner conversations showed the technology existed and the business case to payers did not. I stopped the build on that evidence.

What I learned

A denial was more likely to mean a misread form than an ineligible patient. That is why every denial stayed with a pharmacist.

The ceiling is set by how prescribers submit. A vendor who can tune its model but cannot edit the client’s form is competing on a variable that was never binding.

Access Fragility: where a drug shortage meets a prior authorization.

I wanted to know what happens when a drug has one supplier, sits behind a coverage restriction, and then has a shortage report. I built the dataset from public data, turned it into a product concept, tested it with two potential partners, and stopped when the evidence said the open question was who pays.

One supplier, restricted, short: 187 of 4,465 markets

Canadian drug markets on 2 Sep 2026 · restriction on Ontario’s public plan · shortage reports since 2017

  1. 4,465
    Drug markets marketed in Canada
  2. 2,835
    Supplied by one company, after resolving parents63% of the stage above
  3. 634

  4. 187
    Had a reported shortage since 201729% of the stage above · 108 molecules

For the 108 molecules in those 187 markets, a patient can neither switch product nor get approved quickly.

Listing has been measured; restriction has not. Sizing the 187 needs claims data, purchase audits and private formularies, none of it public.

Does another strength of the same drug settle it?

All 187 markets, by what a shortage alert would tell the plan to do

  • 17: another strength settles it, or nearly
  • 94: the plan can act, with temporary relief or a temporary benefit
  • 76: needs a different drug, by class switch or import

177 of 187 markets have no same-molecule fallback that is both open on the ODB and free of a shortage report in the same window.

Three retro cases

Real shortage reports replayed against the formulary. All three drugs are EAP-only on the ODB.

  • Nothing equivalent marketed

    Pegasys

    peginterferon alfa-2a 180 mcg

    101days

    From the shortage report to Health Canada listing an imported substitute. It arrived without a DIN, and Ontario reaches it only by case-by-case review.

  • The alternative is gated too

    Kevzara

    sarilumab 150 mg

    434days

    From the EU publishing a shortage to the Canadian episode. The closest alternative, tocilizumab, was also EAP-only at the time.

  • Control

    Hydromorph Contin

    hydromorphone CR 24 mg

    2×12mg

    The right alert said to do nothing about coverage. The 24 mg restriction exists for opioid safety, and two 12 mg capsules stayed covered.

Testing it with potential partners

Two conversations moved the project from a dataset to a business question

A health-tech CEO, as mentor

Asked where the action was, then whether this was a painkiller or a vitamin for the buyer.

I redesigned it as signal, then formulary exposure, then an action the plan can take.

Co-founder of a university shortage-forecasting startup

They already had the action step and could take in a plan’s formulary, but had pitched insurers with little success.

The gap was not technology. It was the business case to payers.

The decision

I stopped the build. The open question was who pays, and more code could not answer it.

The partner already had the technology, so building more would have duplicated it. I kept the evidence, the method and a working relationship with the founder.

What I learned

  • Start from the buyer’s pain

    I spent weeks on a rigorous dataset before asking who would pay. Next time the customer conversation comes first.

  • Pitch to whoever carries the cost

    A tool that looks like it replaces someone’s work meets that person’s resistance.

  • Stopping is a decision

    A clear stop, made on evidence, beats a half-finished product.

What Actually Breaks When You Put GenAI into Prior Authorization

Roughly 95 percent of the prior authorization requests we received arrived as faxes or email attachments. Under 5 percent came through electronic prior authorization. Of the fax and email volume, about 30 percent produced structured data clean enough to route into the automated path. Everything else landed in a human queue before a model ever saw it.

More than one in five single-supplier drug markets in Canada is restricted on Ontario’s public formulary, and nearly three in ten of those have had a reported shortage since 2017.
